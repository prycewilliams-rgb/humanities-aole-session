Section 3: The Welsh History Debate — Context and Analysis Adran 3: Y Ddadl Hanes Cymru — Cyd-destun a Dadansoddiad

A Brief Timeline · Llinell Amser Fer

Pre-2015

Welsh history available but optional

Welsh history could be taught but was not mandated. Many schools chose to focus on English or British topics because of available resources, teacher confidence and examination specifications. The result was that the majority of Welsh young people left school with little knowledge of their own country's history.

2015

Political debate intensifies — calls for compulsory Welsh history

Assembly Members, historians and campaigners argue publicly that Welsh history should be made compulsory. BBC Wales reports widespread concern that pupils can study the Nazis and the Tudors in depth but leave school never having heard of Owain Glyndŵr or the Senghennydd disaster. The debate leads directly into the Donaldson Review.

2015–2020

Successful Futures review and CfW development

Professor Graham Donaldson's review, Successful Futures (2015), recommends a wholesale reform of the Welsh curriculum. Welsh history and the Welsh dimension are built into the new Humanities AoLE as a mandatory requirement. Statement 3 specifically addresses identity, heritage and cynefin. The Welsh Government commits to a curriculum in which the story of Wales is central.

2022

CfW introduced — but implementation uneven

The Curriculum for Wales is formally introduced. BBC Wales reports a significant gap between the curriculum's stated ambitions and what is happening in classrooms: many schools lack resources, training and specialist expertise to deliver Welsh history with confidence. The transition from policy to practice proves slower and more difficult than anticipated.

2024

Concern persists — the gap between intent and reality

WalesOnline reports continuing concern from historians, educators and politicians that Welsh young people are still leaving school without adequate knowledge of their own country. The debate now focuses not on whether Welsh history should be taught, but on whether the conditions exist for it to be taught well: trained teachers, quality resources, and departmental confidence in Welsh historical content.

The Argument — Both Sides · Y Ddadl — Y Ddau Ochr

Arguments FOR more Welsh history · O BLAID Arguments that complicate this · Dadleuon cymhleth
Welsh young people have a democratic right to know the history of the country in which they live — its struggles, achievements and contested past. Knowledge of Welsh history is a prerequisite for informed citizenship. / Mae gan bobl ifanc Cymru hawl ddemocrataidd i adnabod hanes eu gwlad.History curriculum time is limited. Adding more Welsh content necessarily means reducing something else — and difficult choices must be made about what constitutes a coherent historical education. / Mae amser cwricwlwm hanes yn gyfyngedig. Mae ychwanegu cynnwys Cymreig yn golygu lleihau rhywbeth arall.
Without Welsh history, learners lack the context to understand contemporary Wales: devolution, the Welsh language, deindustrialisation, patterns of migration and settlement, and Wales's relationship with England and the wider world. / Heb hanes Cymru, mae'r dysgu ar gyfer deall Cymru gyfoes.What counts as ‘Welsh history’ is itself contested. Wales has a diverse, multilingual, multicultural history — and any selection of content involves choices about whose Wales is being taught. / Mae hanes Cymru ei hun yn cwestiynu. Mae unrhyw ddewis o gynnwys yn cynnwys dewisiadau am hanes pwy sy'n cael ei addysgu.
The Welsh language and Welsh identity cannot be fully understood without historical context. The decline of Welsh-speaking communities, the history of language suppression and revival, and the significance of places like Tryweryn are all historical stories. / Ni ellir deall yr iaith Gymraeg a hunaniaeth Gymreig heb gyd-destun hanesyddol.Many history teachers in Wales are not specialist Welsh historians. Mandating Welsh history without investment in teacher training and resources risks poor quality delivery that undermines the subject rather than promoting it. / Nid yw llawer o athrawon hanes yn haneswyr Cymraeg arbenigol. Mae gofynion heb fuddsoddiad mewn hyfforddiant yn risg.
Welsh history includes some of the most vivid and important stories in British and global history — the industrial revolution in South Wales, the Rebecca Riots, the Chartist March, the 1919 race riots in Cardiff, the drowning of Tryweryn — and these deserve to be taught for their historical significance as well as their Welsh context. / Mae hanes Cymru yn cynnwys rhai o'r straeon mwyaf bywiog a phwysig mewn hanes Prydain a byd.The Curriculum for Wales already mandates a Welsh dimension. The problem may not be curriculum design but implementation: school budgets, resource availability, and the confidence and training of teachers. Changing the curriculum again may not solve a problem that is fundamentally about capacity. / Efallai nad yw'r broblem yn ymwneud â dyluniad cwricwlwm ond â gweithredu.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 The CfW Requirement · Gofyniad y CfW

The Curriculum for Wales Humanities AoLE explicitly requires that learners develop a sense of identity, heritage and cynefin (Statement 3). This is not optional — it is a named requirement. Learners must be given consistent exposure to the story of their locality and the story of Wales alongside the wider world. As a history teacher in Wales, teaching Welsh history is part of your professional obligation, not an optional extra. · Mae MDP y Dyniaethau yn y Cwricwlwm i Gymru yn ei gwneud yn ofynnol i ddysgwyr ddatblygu ymdeimlad o hunaniaeth, treftadaeth a chynefin (Datganiad 3).
