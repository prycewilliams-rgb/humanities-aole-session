Section 1: What Does Progression Mean in the Humanities AoLE? Adran 1: Beth Mae Cynnydd yn ei Olygu ym MDP y Dyniaethau?

🇬🇧 English

In the Curriculum for Wales, progression is not simply about covering more content. It describes the ways in which learners become more sophisticated thinkers, more independent enquirers, and more confident users of disciplinary knowledge and skills over time. The Humanities AoLE has five statutory Principles of Progression and five Progression Steps that together define what it means to make progress from ages 5 to 16.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Yng Nghwricwlwm i Gymru, nid yw cynnydd yn ymwneud yn syml â chwmpasu mwy o gynnwys. Mae'n disgrifio'r ffyrdd y mae dysgwyr yn dod yn feddylwyr mwy soffistigedig, yn ymholwyr mwy annibynnol, ac yn ddefnyddwyr mwy hyderus o wybodaeth a sgiliau disgyblaethol dros amser.

📌 Three Interconnected Structures · Tair Strwythur Cydgysylltiedig

Principles of Progression (mandatory) — five statutory statements describing how learners make progress in Humanities. These apply across the whole 3–16 continuum and must be reflected in your curriculum design. / Egwyddorion Cynnydd (gorfodol) — pum datganiad statudol yn disgrifio sut mae dysgwyr yn gwneud cynnydd.

Progression Steps (PS1–PS5) — five reference points that broadly correspond to ages 5, 8, 11, 14 and 16. They describe what learners should be achieving at each point across the Humanities disciplines. They are not rigid thresholds — learners progress at different paces. / Camau Cynnydd (CS1–CS5) — pum pwynt cyfeirio sy'n cyfateb yn fras i oedrannau 5, 8, 11, 14 a 16.

Descriptions of Learning — detailed statements within each Progression Step showing what progress looks like for each Statement of What Matters. These guide your planning, not your assessment. / Disgrifiadau Dysgu — datganiadau manwl o fewn pob Cam Cynnydd yn dangos sut olwg sydd ar gynnydd.

🏠 Why does this matter for your teaching? · Pam mae hyn yn bwysig i'ch addysgu?

  • You must plan your Humanities curriculum so that learners progressively deepen their knowledge, skills and understanding — not just encounter new content / Rhaid i chi gynllunio eich cwricwlwm Dyniaethau fel bod dysgwyr yn dyfnhau'n raddol eu gwybodaeth, sgiliau a dealltwriaeth
  • You need to understand where your learners are on the 3–16 continuum and plan learning that moves them forward — not just delivers content / Rhaid i chi ddeall ble mae eich dysgwyr ar y continwwm ac gynllunio dysgu sy'n eu symud ymlaen
  • Progression in Humanities is not linear: revisiting the same concepts with increasing depth and complexity is itself a form of progression / Nid yw cynnydd yn llinol: mae dychwelyd at yr un cysyniadau gyda dyfnder a chymhlethdod cynyddol yn ffurf o gynnydd
  • Assessment should help you understand where a learner is and what they need next — not measure them against a fixed list of content / Dylai asesu eich helpu i ddeall ble mae dysgwr ac yr hyn sydd ei angen arno nesaf

Section 2: The Five Mandatory Principles of Progression Adran 2: Y Pum Egwyddor Gynnydd Gorfodol

📌 Statutory status · Statws statudol

These five principles are mandatory — taken directly from Hwb (hwb.gov.wales/curriculum-for-wales/humanities/principles-of-progression). Your curriculum design must reflect all five. They are not aspirational guidelines; they are the required shape of progression in Humanities. / Mae'r pum egwyddor hyn yn orfodol. Rhaid i'ch dyluniad cwricwlwm adlewyrchu'r pump i gyd.

1
Increasing effectiveness as a learner
Cynyddu effeithiolrwydd fel dysgwr

As learners make progress within this Area, they will be asking increasingly sophisticated enquiry questions. They will show a greater independence in finding suitable information, making informed predictions and hypotheses, and making judgements including about reliability and utility. They will also become more able to effectively work with others, especially, but not limited to, taking part in social action.

🔨 What this means for your planning Progression is not just about what learners know — it is about what they can do with that knowledge. Plan for learners to take greater ownership of enquiry questions, to choose their own sources, and to evaluate evidence with increasing independence. A Year 7 pupil might be given a question; a Year 10 pupil should be framing their own. / Nid yw cynnydd yn ymwneud yn unig â'r hyn y mae dysgwyr yn ei wybod — mae'n ymwneud â'r hyn y gallant ei wneud gyda'r wybodaeth honno.
2
Increasing breadth and depth of knowledge
Cynyddu lled a dyfnder gwybodaeth

Progression in the Humanities Area is demonstrated by learners engaging with an increasing breadth and depth of knowledge and underlying concepts. Learners increasingly develop the capacity to organise and make links across propositional knowledge, to identify and develop more powerful concepts related to the area of study, and to make supported judgements in more complex contexts. Learners connect new ideas and information to knowledge acquired from previous learning from within and outside school and use it to build an increasingly clear and coherent understanding of the world around them.

🔨 What this means for your planning This is not about more facts — it is about deeper conceptual understanding. A younger learner might understand that events have causes; an older learner understands that causation is multi-layered, contested and constructed. Plan for revisiting concepts (change, significance, enquiry) with increasing complexity across years. / Nid yw hyn am fwy o ffeithiau — mae am ddealltwriaeth gysyniadol ddyfnach.
3
Deepening understanding of the ideas and disciplines within Areas
Dyfnhau dealltwriaeth o syniadau a disgyblaethau o fewn Meysydd

Progression within this Area is demonstrated in the early stages as learners experience holistic approaches to exploring the world around them and are supported in shaping an understanding of themselves in the world. Learners will move on to more focused awareness of the lives of others, in their own social context, elsewhere in the world and in different eras. As they move through the continuum of learning, learners have an increased understanding of the defining features of the constituent disciplines (including history; geography; religion, values and ethics; business studies and social studies) and how these can be brought together to provide different lenses through which to view issues and address questions or problems.

🔨 What this means for your planning Early learners experience Humanities holistically. As they progress, they begin to understand what makes History different from Geography, and RVE different from both. By KS4, learners should understand what disciplinary thinking looks like in their subject — how historians use evidence, how geographers use fieldwork, how RVE scholars engage with philosophical and ethical arguments. Plan for disciplinary as well as substantive progression. / Mae dysgwyr cynnar yn profi'r Dyniaethau yn gyfannol. Wrth iddynt symud ymlaen, maent yn dechrau deall beth sy'n gwneud Hanes yn wahanol i Ddaraearyddiaeth.
4
Refinement and growing sophistication in the use and application of skills
Mireinio a chynyddu soffistigeiddrwydd yn y defnydd a chymhwysiad o sgiliau

As learners experience, understand and apply increasingly complex concepts, they show an increasing accuracy and fluency in using a variety of skills identified in the descriptions of learning and statements of what matters. As they progress, learners will be continually refining and developing a growing sophistication of key disciplinary skills, including those relating to enquiry such as framing questions and using evidence to construct and support an answer, and relating that to representation and interpretation of enquiry results. Progression in this Area is demonstrated through an ability to work with an increasing number and sophistication of sources of information, and a growing understanding of how to resolve contradictory or conflicting accounts.

🔨 What this means for your planning Skills are not one-off activities to be taught then ticked off. Framing enquiry questions, evaluating sources, interpreting data, weighing competing arguments — these are skills that must be developed progressively across years with increasing complexity. Plan for learners to encounter more, and more challenging, sources over time; and to handle contradiction and conflict in evidence with increasing confidence. / Nid yw sgiliau'n weithgareddau untro. Rhaid eu datblygu'n raddol ar draws blynyddoedd gyda chymhlethdod cynyddol.
5
Making connections and transferring learning into new contexts
Creu cysylltiadau a throsglwyddo dysgu i gyd-destunau newydd

Progression in this Area is also characterised through more sophisticated use of relevant skills and the growing ability to transfer existing skills and knowledge into new, and increasingly unfamiliar contexts. As learners progress, they will be able to make links within and between periods and places, identifying similarities and differences, changes and continuities, and use the understanding of concepts to identify connections between new and previous learning. With greater understanding of the world, of other people and their values, in different times, places and circumstances, of their environment and how it has been shaped, learners will demonstrate greater ability to influence events by exercising informed and responsible citizenship.

🔨 What this means for your planning The hallmark of deep progression is transfer: a learner who can take what they know about change in Welsh industrial history and apply that conceptual thinking to a completely new context has genuinely progressed. Plan for tasks that require learners to make connections across topics, across disciplines, and from the past to the present. Cynefin — the sense of place — is a key vehicle for this in Wales. / Nodwedd cynnydd dwfn yw trosglwyddiad: dysgwr sy'n gallu cymryd yr hyn y maent yn ei wybod am newid mewn hanes diwydiannol Cymreig a chymhwyso'r meddwl cysyniadol hwnnw i gyd-destun newydd.

Section 3: Progression Steps 1–5 — What Do They Look Like? Adran 3: Camau Cynnydd 1–5 — Sut Olwg Sydd Arnynt?

🔎 Important note about Progression Steps · Nodyn pwysig

Progression Steps are reference points, not assessment levels. They describe what learners should broadly be able to do at ages 5, 8, 11, 14 and 16. Learners progress at different rates — a PS4 learner is not necessarily in Year 9. As a secondary Humanities teacher, PS3, PS4 and PS5 are most relevant to you, but understanding the full continuum helps you know where your learners have come from and where they are going. / Mae'r Camau Cynnydd yn bwyntiau cyfeirio, nid lefelau asesu.

PS1

Progression Step 1 — broadly age 5 · Cam Cynnydd 1 — tua 5 oed Nursery & Year 1–2 · Meithrin a Blwyddyn 1–2

Learners at PS1 are beginning their journey through the Humanities. They explore the world around them in a holistic, experiential way. The focus is on themselves, their immediate environment and the people close to them. / Mae dysgwyr ar CS1 yn dechrau eu taith drwy'r Dyniaethau. Maent yn archwilio'r byd o'u cwmpas mewn ffordd gyfannol a phrofiadol.

Enquiry What learners can broadly do / Yr hyn y gall dysgwyr ei wneud yn fras
Begin to ask simple questions about the world around them and their immediate experience. Explore their local environment and community. Notice similarities and differences between themselves and others. Begin to understand that things change over time in simple ways. Relate to stories about people in the past and people in other places.
Cynefin Identity and belonging / Hunaniaeth a pherthyn
Begin to develop a sense of themselves, their family, their local community and their place in Wales. Explore what makes their local area special — their cynefin. Begin to understand that other people may have different traditions, stories and beliefs.
PS2

Progression Step 2 — broadly age 8 · Cam Cynnydd 2 — tua 8 oed Year 3–4 · Blwyddyn 3–4

Learners at PS2 are developing their ability to ask questions and use a range of sources. They begin to place events in time, explore their local area in more detail, and consider why people hold different beliefs and values. / Mae dysgwyr ar CS2 yn datblygu eu gallu i ofyn cwestiynau a defnyddio ystod o ffynonellau.

Enquiry What learners can broadly do / Yr hyn y gall dysgwyr ei wneud yn fras
Ask and respond to questions about the world around them, the past and other places. Use simple sources of evidence to find out about the past and present. Place events in a simple chronological order and understand that things change over time. Recognise that there can be different accounts of events.
Geography Place and environment / Lle ac amgylchedd
Describe their local environment and make comparisons with other places. Understand that the physical environment affects how people live. Begin to use maps and geographical tools to find out about places in Wales and beyond.
RVE Beliefs and values / Credoau a gwerthoedd
Show awareness that people have different beliefs, values and traditions. Begin to explore religious and non-religious worldviews and ask questions about meaning and purpose. Understand that their community in Wales includes people with different beliefs and traditions.
PS3

Progression Step 3 — broadly age 11 · Cam Cynnydd 3 — tua 11 oed Year 5–7 · Blwyddyn 5–7

This is a pivotal progression step — learners begin to engage with the disciplines of History, Geography and RVE more distinctly. They use a wider range of sources with growing confidence, place events in broader chronological contexts, and begin to understand that evidence can be interpreted in different ways. / Dyma gam cynnydd allweddol — mae dysgwyr yn dechrau ymgysylltu yn fwy penodol â disgyblaethau Hanes, Daearyddiaeth a GAE.

History What learners can broadly do / Yr hyn y gall dysgwyr ei wneud yn fras
Frame and investigate enquiry questions about the past, including Welsh history. Use a range of sources of evidence, evaluating their usefulness and reliability. Understand that historical accounts can differ and begin to explain why. Place events in a broad chronological framework, identifying causes and consequences. Understand key historical concepts including change, continuity, significance and causation.
Geography Place and environment / Lle ac amgylchedd
Investigate geographical questions about places and environments in Wales and beyond. Use maps, data and fieldwork to gather, represent and interpret geographical evidence. Understand physical and human processes and their interconnections. Begin to evaluate different perspectives on environmental and human issues.
RVE Beliefs and values / Credoau a gwerthoedd
Investigate different religious and non-religious worldviews and their impact on people's lives. Explore ethical questions from a range of perspectives. Understand that Welsh society includes people of diverse religious, cultural and non-religious backgrounds. Develop respectful and informed attitudes towards religious and non-religious worldviews.
Cynefin Wales and local identity / Cymru a hunaniaeth leol
Develop a sense of their own identity and place within Wales, its history and its communities. Connect their learning to their local cynefin and to the broader story of Wales. Understand the diverse communities, languages and cultures that make up Wales today.
PS4

Progression Step 4 — broadly age 14 · Cam Cynnydd 4 — tua 14 oed Year 8–9 · Blwyddyn 8–9

At PS4, learners engage with increased sophistication in disciplinary thinking. They evaluate evidence more critically, construct more complex arguments, and apply conceptual understanding to new and unfamiliar contexts. / Ar CS4, mae dysgwyr yn ymgysylltu gyda meddwl disgyblaethol gyda mwy o soffistigeiddrwydd.

History What learners can broadly do / Yr hyn y gall dysgwyr ei wneud yn fras
Frame increasingly sophisticated historical enquiry questions and investigate them independently. Critically evaluate a range of sources, understanding the relationship between provenance, context, purpose and utility. Construct sustained historical arguments supported by evidence. Understand that historical interpretations are shaped by the context in which they are produced. Apply second-order concepts (causation, change, significance, evidence, interpretations, historical perspective) to complex historical situations, including Welsh and global history.
Geography Place and environment / Lle ac amgylchedd
Conduct geographical investigations using a range of quantitative and qualitative methods. Analyse and evaluate data, including maps, graphs, fieldwork data and digital sources. Understand complex physical and human geographical processes and their interrelationships. Evaluate different geographical perspectives on complex issues including climate change, migration and urbanisation, with Welsh and global contexts.
RVE Beliefs and values / Credoau a gwerthoedd
Analyse and evaluate diverse religious and non-religious worldviews with increasing sophistication. Engage with complex ethical questions using a range of philosophical and theological frameworks. Understand the diversity of belief and practice within and across religious traditions. Apply ethical reasoning to contemporary issues including human rights, environmental ethics and social justice, in Welsh and global contexts.
PS5

Progression Step 5 — broadly age 16 · Cam Cynnydd 5 — tua 16 oed Year 10–11 · Blwyddyn 10–11

At PS5, learners demonstrate the highest level of Humanities understanding expected at 16. They engage with complex disciplinary concepts with independence and nuance, and show the ability to transfer learning to new contexts and exercise informed citizenship. / Ar CS5, mae dysgwyr yn dangos y lefel uchaf o ddealltwriaeth Dyniaethau a ddisgwylir yn 16 oed.

History What learners can broadly do / Yr hyn y gall dysgwyr ei wneud yn fras
Independently frame, investigate and reach supported conclusions on complex historical enquiries. Critically analyse and evaluate a wide range of primary and secondary sources, understanding how context shapes historical knowledge. Construct complex, nuanced historical arguments that acknowledge contradictory evidence, multiple causation and different interpretations. Understand how historiographical perspectives have changed over time, and how the history of Wales connects to and informs understanding of the wider world.
Geography Synthesis and action / Syntesu a gweithredu
Conduct and evaluate sophisticated geographical investigations, justifying methodological choices. Synthesise complex geographical evidence to reach well-supported conclusions on issues of local, national and global significance. Understand the complex interactions between physical and human geography, and between Wales, the UK and the wider world. Engage with geographical issues as an informed citizen, including through social action.
RVE Critical engagement / Ymgysylltiad beirniadol
Critically and empathetically engage with a wide range of religious and non-religious worldviews. Construct and evaluate complex ethical arguments, demonstrating awareness of philosophical frameworks and their limitations. Understand the political, social and historical contexts that shape religious and non-religious belief. Exercise informed and responsible citizenship in a religiously diverse Welsh and global society.
Transfer Citizenship and action / Dinasyddiaeth a gweithredu
Apply Humanities knowledge and skills to new, unfamiliar and complex situations. Make connections across disciplines, periods and places. Demonstrate ability to influence events by exercising informed and responsible citizenship at local, national and global levels. Show a clear understanding of their own identity within Wales and within the wider world.

Section 4: Progression Across the Three Humanities Disciplines Adran 4: Cynnydd ar Draws Tair Disgyblaeth y Dyniaethau

Each of the three main Humanities disciplines has its own progression pathway — but they share the same five principles and five progression steps. This section shows how progression looks different in History, Geography and RVE at key stages. / Mae gan bob un o dair prif ddisgyblaethol y Dyniaethau ei llwybr cynnydd ei hun — ond maent yn rhannu'r un pum egwyddor a phum cam cynnydd.

The concept of ENQUIRY across PS3–PS5 · Y cysyniad o YMHOLIAD ar draws CS3–CS5

🕼 History · Hanes
  • PS3: Investigate given enquiry questions; evaluate sources provided; understand that accounts differ
  • PS4: Frame own enquiry questions; independently evaluate a range of sources; understand how interpretations are shaped by context
  • PS5: Conduct independent complex enquiries; analyse historiographical change; connect Welsh history to world history
🌎 Geography · Daearyddiaeth
  • PS3: Investigate geographical questions using provided maps, data, fieldwork; understand that geographical processes are interconnected
  • PS4: Conduct own geographical investigations; evaluate methodological choices; analyse complex human and physical processes
  • PS5: Design and evaluate sophisticated investigations; synthesise complex evidence; engage as informed citizen on global issues
☮ RVE · GAE
  • PS3: Investigate different worldviews; explore ethical questions; understand diversity within Welsh society
  • PS4: Analyse worldviews critically; apply philosophical frameworks; engage with complex contemporary ethical issues
  • PS5: Construct complex ethical arguments; evaluate philosophical frameworks; exercise informed citizenship in diverse society

What progression looks like in practice · Sut olwg sydd ar gynnydd yn ymarferol

PS3 (age ~11): A learner can identify and list causes of an event (e.g. the Rebecca Riots). They can sort causes into categories (economic, political, social) and explain which they think was most important with some reference to evidence.

PS4 (age ~14): A learner understands that causation is complex and contested. They can construct a sustained argument about causation, weighing different types of evidence, acknowledging that historians have prioritised different causes, and explaining why context shapes judgements about significance.

What has progressed? Not just more knowledge of the Rebecca Riots — deeper conceptual understanding of how causation works as a historical concept, and greater independence in applying that understanding to argument.

PS3 (age ~11): A learner can read a climate graph for Cardiff, describe the pattern of rainfall and temperature, and explain in simple terms why Cardiff receives more rainfall than eastern England.

PS4 (age ~14): A learner can design a fieldwork method to investigate rainfall patterns, collect and process data, choose appropriate graphical representations, and evaluate the reliability and validity of their own methodology. They can connect climate patterns to human and environmental issues (flooding, agriculture, tourism) in Wales.

What has progressed? From reading provided data to designing and evaluating their own data collection. From description to analysis and evaluation. From a simple task to an independently conducted geographical investigation.

PS3 (age ~11): A learner can explain what different religious traditions teach about care for the environment, identify similarities and differences between them, and express a personal view on environmental responsibility.

PS5 (age ~16): A learner can construct a complex ethical argument about climate justice, drawing on utilitarian, deontological and virtue ethics frameworks alongside religious teachings. They can evaluate the strengths and limitations of different ethical approaches, acknowledge competing claims and reach a nuanced, supported conclusion. They understand the political and economic contexts that shape responses to climate change globally and in Wales.

What has progressed? From knowing what traditions teach to applying and evaluating philosophical frameworks. From a personal opinion to a constructed, evidenced ethical argument. From description of diversity to critical engagement with it.

Section 5: Planning for Progression in Your Teaching Adran 5: Cynllunio ar gyfer Cynnydd yn Eich Addysgu

📌 The planning question · Y cwestiwn cynllunio

For every lesson and unit you plan, ask: "How does this learning move my learners forward on the 3–16 continuum?" If you cannot answer this, the learning may be covering content without developing progression. / Ar gyfer pob gwers ac uned rydych chi'n ei gynllunio, gofynnwch: "Sut mae'r dysgu hwn yn symud fy nysgwyr ymlaen ar y continwwm 3–16?"

Key planning principles · Egwyddorion cynllunio allweddol

✓ Plan for depth, not coverage

Returning to the same concept (causation, significance, enquiry) with increasing depth and sophistication IS progression. Do not feel you need to cover everything — deep engagement with a smaller number of powerful ideas is more valuable than surface coverage of many. / Dychwelyd at yr un cysyniad gyda dyfnder cynyddol yw cynnydd.

✓ Plan for increasing independence

At PS3, learners work with provided enquiry questions and sources. By PS4, they are framing their own questions and choosing their own sources. By PS5, they are designing their own investigations. Plan the gradual release of responsibility deliberately. / Cynllunio datgeliad graddol o gyfrifoldeb yn fwriadol.

✓ Plan for transfer

Include tasks that require learners to apply what they have learned to unfamiliar contexts. A Geography student who can apply knowledge of flooding causes learned in Wales to a case study in Bangladesh is demonstrating genuine progression. / Cynnwys tasgau sy'n ei gwneud yn ofynnol i ddysgwyr gymhwyso'r hyn y maent wedi'i ddysgu i gyd-destunau anghyfarwydd.

✓ Plan for connections across time and place

The CfW explicitly expects learners to make connections within and between periods and places. Plan for learners to connect their Welsh cynefin to wider national and global stories. Explicitly ask: how does this connect to what we studied before? / Cynllunio i ddysgwyr gysylltu eu cynefin Cymreig â straeon cenedlaethol a byd-eang ehangach.
