How your school time is structured

Your time in school isn't just about teaching lessons. It's carefully planned to help you build expertise gradually.

The four types of clinical practice activity

Each day in school combines different activities. The balance changes week by week as you develop.

🔬

Research & Enquiry (R&E)

Observing, reading, gathering evidence, team planning, Welsh language development, and professional conversations. This is the backbone of your learning.

🎤

Independent Teaching

Planning and delivering lessons on your own. One day = no more than 4 sessions or 4 hours — whichever is less.

🤝

Team Planning & Teaching

Planning and delivering alongside your mentor. You might take the starter; they manage behaviour — or vice versa. You must both agree the learning intentions first.

📋

PPA (Planning, Prep & Assessment)

Dedicated time to plan, prepare resources, and mark/assess pupil work.

How the balance shifts across Clinical Practice 1

At the start, most of your time is R&E. By week 14, you're teaching two full days independently. This is deliberate — you build skills before you carry full responsibility.

💡 Why so much R&E at the start?

If you're struggling with timing starter tasks, it's far more effective to spend R&E time watching experienced colleagues deliver starters and then practise it several times — rather than only addressing it whilst managing a whole lesson.

Who's involved in your placement?

You (Student Teacher) Mentor Senior Mentor University Tutor
🧑‍🏫 Your responsibilities as student teacher ▾
  • Sign and abide by the Student Teacher Code of Conduct
  • Maintain your Professional Learning Portfolio (PLP) and give all parties access
  • Attend and engage with mentor and senior mentor meetings weekly
  • Take professional responsibility for your own learning and progress
  • Take on a full professional role — meetings, INSET, duties, parents' evenings, extra-curricular
  • Remain on school premises for the entire school day
🤝 Your mentor's role ▾
Your mentor is your first line of support. They will meet with you weekly, provide formal written feedback through Learning & Teaching Observation forms, support your planning and R&E, and assess your progress at KAPs. Think of them as a teacher educator — not just a supervisor.
🏫 Your senior mentor's role ▾
The senior mentor runs weekly whole-group professional learning sessions with all student teachers in the school. They quality-assure your mentor's assessments, monitor your overall progress, and facilitate R&E opportunities. They are also responsible for ensuring you receive your full entitlement.

Research & Enquiry

R&E isn't extra work on top of teaching — it's how you develop the thinking that makes your teaching better.

What counts as R&E?

R&E brings many types of knowledge to bear on your practice: observation, team teaching, professional conversation, reading, and hands-on practice of specific skills.

Bespoke R&E activities

You and your mentor also identify activities tailored to your specific needs. Here are the main types:

🏋️ Deliberate practice▾
Repeatedly practise a specific aspect of teaching (e.g. explanations, learning intentions) in a low-stakes environment — in a classroom with pupils, in a mentor meeting, with peers, or even filmed on your phone. This is research-backed as one of the most effective ways to develop teaching skill.
👁️ Focused observation▾
Observing an experienced colleague, a peer, or individual learners — focused on one specific aspect (e.g. questioning techniques, managing low-level disruption, adaptive teaching). Always agree the focus in advance and use it to inform your next teaching.
📚 Engaging with sources▾
Reading, watching, listening — from academic journals to blogs, podcasts, and internal resources. Your mentor can recommend materials relevant to your areas for development.
💬 Learning conversations▾
A focused conversation with a specialist colleague — for example, the ALNCo about additional learning needs, or the Welsh coordinator about language development. Your mentor can help you identify who to speak to.
📊 Gathering & analysing data▾
Collecting information about learner needs or school priorities. This feeds directly into your university assignments and can lead to practical changes in your planning and teaching.

🌿 Welsh Language Development (8 hours per placement)

You'll work with the school's Welsh Language Coordinator to develop both your own Welsh skills and your pupils'. Activities include: lesson observations where Welsh is used across the curriculum, Welsh learning walks, peer observations, and attending events like the school Eisteddfod. Document your evidence under the Personal Skills tab on your PLP.

📝 Important: Reflective models on the PLP

The Driscoll model prompts you to return later and complete the "This is what?" phase — assessing the impact on learning. Your mentor will remind you. Use reflective models for the big, complex questions — not every small issue.

…helps it grow · 4 elements

Pedagogy is the largest cluster — it's where most of your day-to-day focus will be. The other four standards support and extend it.

The Continuum of Effective Practice

You'll be assessed against a four-stage continuum. Most people start at Unaware — that's expected and normal.

Unaware
  • Limited impact
  • Erratic
  • Over-reliant
  • Lacking rigour
  • Superficial
  • Lacking confidence
Aware
  • Some impact
  • Inconsistent
  • With support
  • Requires further rigour
  • Fairly superficial
  • Developing confidence
Capable ✓ QTS
  • (Mainly) impactful
  • (Mainly) consistent
  • (Mainly) independent
  • Mainly rigorous
  • (Mainly) thorough
  • Confident
QTS+
  • Strong impact
  • Consistent
  • Shows initiative
  • Rigorous
  • In depth
  • Innovative

Your target: reach "Capable" by the end of CP2

Capable is the minimum for QTS. It means you're mainly consistent, mainly independent, and applying what you know with confidence. You don't need to be perfect — you need to be capable and growing.

Assessment milestones

There are four Key Assessment Points (KAPs) — formal Progress Reviews where your mentor assesses your progress against the PSTL.

KAP 1 — CP1 Mid-point
CP1 mid-point (PGCE week 10)

All 10 critical descriptors: at least "Aware"

If any critical descriptor is still at "Unaware", a Concern over Progress (CoP) process begins. Prepare by self-assessing your PLP and attending your Progress Review ready to discuss evidence.

KAP 2 — CP1 Summative
CP1 end (weeks 14–15 window)

Majority of Pedagogy elements + all critical descriptors: at least "Aware"

Not meeting this threshold means a recommendation of Fail to the exam board. You must pass CP1 to proceed to CP2.

KAP 3 — CP2 Mid-point
CP2 mid-point (PGCE week 26)

All 32 descriptors: at least "Aware" · All 10 critical descriptors: at least "Capable"

By this point you should be moving confidently towards Capable across the board. If not, the CoP process applies.

KAP 4 — CP2 Summative
CP2 end (PGCE week 35)

All 32 descriptors: at least "Capable" = QTS awarded

This is the finish line. Capable across everything = QTS. Some students will be working at QTS+ by this point — celebrated on the form. If eligible for enrichment, an early KAP4 is held in week 30.

How Progress Reviews work

Before each Progress Review you should self-assess on the PLP rubric and complete your synthesis of progress. Bring evidence. Your mentor will then discuss with you and make final judgements. It's a dialogue, not a one-way verdict.

Written feedback you'll receive

📝

L&T Observation Forms

7 across the programme (4 in CP1, 3 in CP2). Completed by mentor after formal lesson observations, using the PLP.

📊

Progress Review Forms

4 times (at each KAP). Covers all 32 elements. Informs your development priorities going forward.

📓

Blue Book (Formative Log)

Informal weekly feedback from your mentor. Keep it with you in school every day. Read it, reflect, and respond to it actively.

Enrichment (for eligible students)

Student teachers who meet QTS standard early can undertake up to 15 enrichment days (weeks 32–35), focusing on innovation and leadership. An early KAP4 is held in week 30. Your focus is negotiated bespoke with your mentor and tutor, and feeds into your Career Entry Profile (CEP).
