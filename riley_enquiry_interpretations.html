Part 1: What Is a Good Enquiry Question? Rhan 1: Beth Yw Cwestiwn Ymholi Da? — 45 minutes

🇬🇧 English

Michael Riley's 2000 article in Teaching History 99 remains one of the most influential pieces on enquiry planning in history education. He uses the metaphor of a 'history garden' — enquiry questions as seeds that, carefully chosen and positioned, grow into coherent, rigorous and motivating learning sequences.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Mae erthygl Michael Riley yn Teaching History 99 (2000) yn parhau i fod yn un o'r darnau mwyaf dylanwadol ar gynllunio ymholiadau mewn addysg hanes. Mae'n defnyddio metaffor 'gardd hanes' — mae cwestiynau ymholi fel hadau, a phan gaiff eu dewis a'u lleoli'n ofalus, maent yn tyfu i fod yn ddilyniant dysgu cydlynol.

"A planning device for knitting together a sequence of lessons, so that all the learning activities — teacher exposition, narrative, source-work, role-play, plenary — all move toward the resolution of an interesting historical problem by means of substantial motivating activity at the end."

— Dr Michael Riley, Teaching History 99 (2000)

1
Discuss in pairs / Trafodwch mewn parau (5 mins)

From Riley's definition, discuss: What does it tell us an enquiry question is NOT? What does 'interesting historical problem' actually mean? What 'substantial motivating activity' have you seen or used? / O ddiffiniad Riley: Beth mae'n ei ddweud wrthym nad yw cwestiwn ymholi DDIM yn ei wneud?

Three Criteria for a Good Individual Enquiry · Tair Maen Prawf

1

Captures Interest & Imagination · Dal Diddordeb

Must create a genuine puzzle — not just a title. 'Did the towns make people free?' is provocative and specific. 'What was life like in towns?' is neither. · Rhaid creu pos dilys — nid dim ond teitl. Mae 'A wnaeth y trefi wneud pobl yn rhydd?' yn heriol ac yn benodol.

2

Historical Thinking at the Forefront · Meddwl Hanesyddol

Every good enquiry foregrounds a second-order concept: causation, change & continuity, evidential understanding, or interpretations — embedded in the question itself, not bolted on. · Rhaid i bob ymholiad da flaenoriaethu cysyniad ail-orchymyn.

3

Substantial Outcome Activity · Gweithgaredd Canlyniad

Pupils must be able to genuinely answer the question at the end — not just write about the topic. The outcome generates real debate and demonstrates real understanding. · Rhaid i ddisgyblion allu ateb y cwestiwn yn ddilys ar y diwedd.

Weak vs Strong: The Towns Example · Gwan vs Cryf

❌ Weak · Gwan
"What was life like in the towns?"
  • Merely descriptive — not historically rigorous
  • No puzzle or intellectual challenge
  • Does not foreground any second-order concept
  • Will not excite lower or higher attainers
  • Drifts into antiquarianism
  • No criteria for choosing or prioritising information
✅ Strong · Cryf
"Did the towns make people free?"
  • Carries a specific, surprising puzzle
  • Foregrounds a genuine historical tension
  • Pupils fascinated by questions of rules and freedom
  • Clear criteria for information selection
  • Allows nuance: free for whom? when? in what ways?
  • Connects to pupils' own sense of fairness and rights
2
Pair task — improve this question / Tasg pâr — gwella'r cwestiwn hwn

"What was life like in the towns?" — Which of the three criteria does it fail? What is wrong with it historically? Reframe it twice — once for each of two different second-order concepts. / Pa un o'r tair maen prawf y mae'n methu? Beth sy'n hanesyddol anghywir? Ail-framiwch ef ddwywaith.

💡 The Key Insight / Y Prif Fewnwelediad

Question 1 — "Was the Treaty of Versailles fair?" asks pupils to BE the moral judge. It invites anachronistic judgement — pupils will give a verdict based on present-day values, not historical understanding.

Question 2 — "Why have some people thought that the Treaty of Versailles was not fair?" asks pupils to UNDERSTAND why people judged differently. It opens up interpretations — different groups, different perspectives, different moments in time.

The shift from 'judge' to 'understand why people judged' is fundamental to all interpretations work. · Mae'r symudiad o 'farnu' i 'ddeall pam roedd pobl yn barnu yn wahanol' yn sylfaenol i waith dehongliadau.

Part 3: A Worked Example — Victorian Child Labour Rhan 3: Enghraifft Weithiol — Llafur Plant Fictoraidd — 50 minutes

🇬🇧 The Enquiry Sequence

This worked example shows how a carefully sequenced enquiry builds from visual stimulus through primary source to historical interpretation. The overarching question sharpens over the sequence, eventually becoming an interpretations question focused on a specific filmmaker's choices.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Y Dilyniant Ymholi

Mae'r enghraifft weithiol hon yn dangos sut mae dilyniant ymholi sydd wedi'i gynllunio'n ofalus yn adeiladu o ysgogiad gweledol drwy ffynhonnell gynradd i ddehongliad hanesyddol. Mae'r cwestiwn gorgyffwrdd yn miniogi dros y dilyniant.

The Enquiry Questions · Y Cwestiynau Ymholi

"What was life really like in the Victorian countryside?"

...sharpening to the interpretations question / ...yn miniogi i'r cwestiwn dehongliadau:

"What makes the start of Michael Winterbottom's film Jude so powerful?"

The Three-Step Sequence · Y Dilyniant Tair Cam

🖼️

Step 1: The Painting

Creates a question: is this accurate? Pupils are curious before they are informed. 30 seconds — invent a title.

📜

Step 2: Primary Sources

Builds knowledge: individual voices, specific detail, evidential complexity. 1867 parliamentary commission testimonies.

🎬

Step 3: Film Interpretation

Now pupils can evaluate: does this reflect what we know? What choices has the filmmaker made?

Step 1: The Harvest Painting · Cam 1: Y Paentiad Cynhaeaf

🖼️ Activity: Invent a Title / Gweithgaredd: Dyfeisiwch Deitl

Pupils are given 30 seconds to invent a title for a harvest painting. Suggestions included: Harvest Picnic · Rewards for Labour · The Country Idyll · Family Day Out · Contentment · Bringing the Corn

The painting's actual title: 'And the Valleys Also Stand Thick With Corn' (Psalm 23) — message: 'Life in the countryside is good.'

4
In pairs — 2 minutes / Mewn parau — 2 munud

What in the painting suggests 'life in the countryside is good'? What has the painter chosen to include — and what might they have left out? What sources could you use to test whether this painting is accurate? / Beth yn y paentiad sy'n awgrymu 'mae bywyd yn y wlad yn dda'?

Step 2: Parliamentary Commission Evidence 1867 · Cam 2: Tystiolaeth 1867

Riley stressed the importance of building children's knowledge about Victorian child labour before analysing an interpretation. These sources do that work. / Pwysleisiodd Riley bwysigrwydd adeiladu gwybodaeth cyn dadansoddi dehongliad.

James Hoare, aged 13 · 13 oed — Staple Fitzpaine, SomersetParliamentary Commission 1867
"I began work at seven years old driving the plough for my father. Found I could not manage the horses well. Sometimes they knocked me down, never hurt me, ran over me sometimes. I went to school before I worked. After I began working, I did five months at school and the rest of the year I worked. I left school at eleven. I liked school better."
George Wembridge, aged 10 · 10 oed — Staple FitzpaineParliamentary Commission 1867
"Began working at nine years, driving plough; can manage horses and keep pigs. Father is a labourer. Was taken away from school to mind birds. The farmer wanted a boy, he gave me two shillings."
Thomas Hole, aged 16 · 16 oed — Bishops Lydeard, SomersetParliamentary Commission 1867
"I first went to work at eight. Went to regular work at nine, when I began to lead horses. Hours 5.30 am to 7 pm in barley sowing; wheat sowing 6.30 am to 4.30 pm. Never went to school at all."
Maria Hayes, aged 10 · 10 oed — Bishops LydeardParliamentary Commission 1867
"Mother goes out washing, and I stay to take care of the children. One of them is seven years old, the other is four. I can take very good care of them. I like coming to school best."
5
Role play + Discussion / Chwarae Rôl + Trafodaeth

Work in pairs. One person = the parliamentary commissioner. The other = a witness from one of the extracts. Rehearse a 2-minute interview. Then discuss: What were the commissioners interested in? What do the children's own voices add that a summary would not? What questions does this source leave unanswered? / Gweithiwch mewn parau. Un person = y comisiynydd seneddol.

Step 3: Film Interpretation — Winterbottom's Jude (1996) · Cam 3: Dehongliad Ffilm

🎬 Three Viewings · Tair Gwyliadwriaeth

Watch the opening of Winterbottom's 1996 film Jude three times, responding to a different question each time. / Gwyliwch agoriad ffilm Winterbottom tair gwaith, gan ymateb i gwestiwn gwahanol bob tro.

1st viewing · 1af

How does the opening make you feel?

2nd viewing · 2il

How has Winterbottom created this feeling?

3rd viewing · 3ydd

How does the film reflect reality?

Filmmaker's choices · Dewisiadau'r cyfarwyddwr: Black-and-white film · Muted sound · Mist · Lonely child in empty landscape · Deliberate atmosphere

6
Discussion / Trafodaeth

Why does showing the film AFTER the primary sources make such a difference? What does the comparison between evidence and film reveal about what interpretations do? How do follow-up questions like 'How typical was it that children worked alone?' push pupils to evaluate the interpretation against evidence? / Pam mae dangos y ffilm AR ÔL y ffynonellau cynradd yn gwneud cymaint o wahaniaeth?

Part 4: The Coroner's Rolls — A Different Enquiry Rhan 4: Rholiau'r Crwner — Ymholiad Gwahanol — 35 minutes

🇬🇧 English

The Somerset and Dorset Coroner's Rolls (1314–1321) are accidental records of medieval life. They record what people were doing when they died — and in doing so, illuminate the texture of daily existence in ways that chronicle sources rarely can. They are rich, not thin — one vivid individual does more work than a list of facts.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Mae Rholiau Crwner Somerset a Dorset (1314–1321) yn gofnodion damweiniol o fywyd canoloesol. Maent yn cofnodi beth roedd pobl yn ei wneud pan fuont farw — ac wrth wneud hynny, maent yn goleuo gwead bywyd bob dydd mewn ffyrdd y mae ffynonellau cronicl yn anaml yn gallu eu cyflawni.

Somerset County Court — Selected Entries · Cofnodion Dethol

Shearston, 1317Somerset Coroner's Rolls
"Robert de Cornwale went to Haddeleghe in Shearston tithing where, because of hunger and destitution, he fell to the ground and died at once by misadventure."
Nettlecombe, 1317Somerset Coroner's Rolls
"Cristina, a poor child, was at Rowdon seeking alms. She was abandoned in Bromparke in Nettlecombe tithing. They do not know by whom, because she was without a father and mother, and she was aged a year or more. She died at once from weakness and the cold."
Greenham, 1321Somerset Coroner's Rolls
"Ralph (the) Smith wanted to knock down the chimney of his forge with one of his blacksmith's hammers in order to repair it. He hammered at his chimney until it leaned over, but as he went out of the door of his smithy the chimney fell on his head, injuring him fatally."
Wellington, 1316Somerset Coroner's Rolls
"William Horny, coming from the market at Wellington, reached Thornheye and there from hunger and weakness he fell to the ground and died by misadventure."
Runnington, 1316Somerset Coroner's Rolls
"William Consail and John atte Brugge together were coming from Wellington market. At Broadbridge they wanted to ride across the Tone on one mare, but because of the height of the water they were drowned by misadventure."
7
Discussion — 10 mins / Trafodaeth — 10 munud

What do these records tell us about medieval life that other sources might not? What are the limitations of this source? What enquiry question might you build around these records? / Beth mae'r cofnodion hyn yn ei ddweud wrthym am fywyd canoloesol na allai ffynonellau eraill ei ddweud?

1–20 seconds

Opening — grab the viewer's attention / Cipiwch sylw'r gwyliedydd

21–40 seconds

Development — individual people and evidence / Unigolion a thystiolaeth

41–60 seconds

Hook into the programme — what's at stake? / Bachyn i mewn i'r rhaglen

What are you leaving out? After sharing: What choices did different groups make? What do those choices reveal about the nature of interpretation? / Ystyriwch: Pa argraff o fywyd ffermwyr canoloesol yr ydych chi am ei chreu?

Riley's Ten Key Questions · Deg Cwestiwn Allweddol Riley

1

Is this area of content significant? / A yw'r cynnwys hwn yn arwyddocaol?

2

How can we turn this into a rigorous and motivating enquiry question? / Sut gallwn droi hwn yn gwestiwn ymholi trylwyr a chymhellol?

3

Can we focus on individual people? / A allwn ganolbwyntio ar unigolion?

4

How will pupils communicate understanding through an engaging end product? / Sut bydd disgyblion yn cyfathrebu dealltwriaeth?

5

How will we hook them in at the start? / Sut byddwn yn eu dal ar y dechrau?

6

How will we sequence the learning for maximum motivation? / Sut byddwn yn dilyniadu'r dysgu?

7

How can we help pupils choose and use information? / Sut gallwn helpu disgyblion i ddewis a defnyddio gwybodaeth?

8

How can we create activities appealing to different intelligences? / Sut gallwn greu gweithgareddau sy'n apelio at ddeallusrwyddau gwahanol?

9

How will we create 'mini-hooks' to engage learners? / Sut byddwn yn creu 'bachynnau mini'?

10

How can we create rich resources rather than 'grubby gobbets'? / Sut gallwn greu adnoddau cyfoethog?

Your Planning Framework — Five Steps · Eich Fframwaith Cynllunio — Pum Cam

1

The Enquiry Question / Y Cwestiwn Ymholi

Draft three versions — weakest to strongest. Apply the three criteria. Which is most rigorous AND most engaging? / Drafftiwch dair fersiwn — o'r gwannaf i'r cryfaf.

2

The Initial Stimulus / Yr Ysgogiad Cychwynnol

What painting, film clip, object or source will hook pupils? How does it create a question before knowledge? / Pa baentiad, clip ffilm, gwrthrych neu ffynhonnell fydd yn dal disgyblion?

3

Source Sequence / Dilyniant Ffynonellau

What types of source feature in each lesson? How does each one add something different to pupils' understanding? / Pa fathau o ffynhonnell sydd ym mhob gwers?

4

The Interpretations Element / Yr Elfen Dehongliadau

Where in the sequence do you introduce an interpretation? What knowledge do pupils need BEFORE they can evaluate it? / Ble yn y dilyniant ydych chi'n cyflwyno dehongliad?

Six Key Takeaways · Chwe Prif Gasgliad

1
An enquiry question must be historically rigorous — it must foreground a second-order concept and require genuine historical thinking, not description or moral judgement.
Rhaid i gwestiwn ymholi fod yn hanesyddol drylwyr — rhaid iddo flaenoriaethu cysyniad ail-orchymyn a gofyn am feddwl hanesyddol dilys.
2
It must also be genuinely engaging — a puzzle pupils want to solve, not just a title for a unit of work. These two demands can conflict, and resolving that tension is the core skill of enquiry planning.
Rhaid iddo hefyd fod yn wirioneddol ddiddorol — pos y mae disgyblion eisiau ei ddatrys. Gall y ddau alw hyn wrthdaro, ac mae datrys y tensiwn hwnnw yn sgil craidd cynllunio ymholiadau.
3
The initial stimulus matters: a painting, film clip or striking source creates curiosity before knowledge, giving pupils a reason to want that knowledge.
Mae'r ysgogiad cychwynnol yn bwysig: mae paentiad, clip ffilm neu ffynhonnell drawiadol yn creu chwilfrydedd cyn gwybodaeth.
4
Interpretations work best when pupils have already built substantive knowledge — the comparison between evidence and interpretation is only possible if pupils know enough to compare.
Mae gwaith dehongliadau yn gweithio orau pan fydd disgyblion eisoes wedi adeiladu gwybodaeth sylweddol — dim ond os yw disgyblion yn gwybod digon i gymharu y mae'r cymhariaeth yn bosibl.
5
Primary sources should be rich, not thin. One vivid individual (James Hoare, Cristina) does more work than a list of facts.
Dylai ffynonellau cynradd fod yn gyfoethog, nid yn denau. Mae un unigolyn bywiog (James Hoare, Cristina) yn gwneud mwy o waith na rhestr o ffeithiau.
6
Planning collectively matters as much as planning individually — enquiry questions should reinforce each other across a Key Stage, building conceptual fluency over time.
Mae cynllunio ar y cyd yr un mor bwysig â chynllunio'n unigol — dylai cwestiynau ymholi atgyfnerthu ei gilydd ar draws cyfnod allweddol.
