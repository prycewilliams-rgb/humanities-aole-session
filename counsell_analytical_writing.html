Section A: Introduction — Aiming High Adran A: Cyflwyniad — Anelu'n Uchel

📚 About the pamphlet · Am y pamffled

Christine Counsell’s 1997 pamphlet for the Historical Association argues that analytical and discursive writing is both essential to history education and genuinely teachable to pupils of almost all abilities. Written for practising teachers, it combines theoretical principles with practical classroom strategies. This guide summarises its key arguments and asks you to connect them to your developing practice. · Mae pamffled Christine Counsell o 1997 yn dadlau bod ysgrifennu dadansoddol a thraethodol yn hanfodol i addysg hanes ac yn wirioneddol addysgu i ddisgyblion o bron pob gallu.

PDF provided alongside this session · Darparwyd PDF ochr yn ochr — read it alongside this guide. The pamphlet is the primary text; this session is the framework for engaging with it.

“Pupils of almost all abilities can write critically and analytically, but we must teach them.”

— Christine Counsell, Analytical and Discursive Writing (Historical Association, 1997)

🇬🇧 English — The Opening Argument

Counsell opens with two pieces of pupil writing: a high-attaining 12-year-old girl (Zoe) and a lower-attaining boy (Jamie) in the same class, both answering ‘Why did the Normans win the Battle of Hastings?’ Both share the same core characteristics: clear structure, a self-conscious distinction between general claims and supporting detail, and focused relevance throughout.

The point is deliberate and provocative: these results were not accidents. They were achieved through a systematic and holistic approach to progression in historical communication, implemented over a long period of time.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg — Y Ddadl Agoriadol

Mae Counsell yn agor gyda dau ddarn o waith ysgrifennu disgyblion: merch 12 oed o gyrhaeddiad uchel a bachgen o gyrhaeddiad is yn yr un dosbarth. Mae gan y ddau yr un nodweddion craidd: strwythur clir, gwahaniaeth ymwybodol rhwng honiadau cyffredinol a manylion cymhorthol, a pherthnasedd trwy gydol y cyfan.

Mae'r pwynt yn fwriadol a heriol: nid damweiniau oedd y canlyniadau hyn. Fe'u cyflawnwyd drwy ddull systematig a chyfannol o ddilyniant mewn cyfathrebu hanesyddol, dros gyfnod hir o amser.

🎯 Central Claim · Prif Honiad

Analytical writing is not an outcome that pupils either have or lack. It is a skill that requires — and responds to — explicit, systematic, structured teaching. Without such teaching, higher attainers may not reach their potential, and lower attainers are almost certain not to. / Nid canlyniad yw ysgrifennu dadansoddol y mae disgyblion naill ai’n ei feddu neu’n ei ddiffygio. Mae’n sgil sy’n gofyn am addysgu penodol, systematig a strwythuredig.

🔥 Counsell’s Challenge to Common Practice · Her Counsell i Arfer Cyffredin

  • The growth of the source ‘gobbet’ in textbooks has encouraged formulaic short answers — ‘here is a proposition, now give me a reaction’ — at the expense of joined-up thinking
  • Lower attainers asked simply to ‘use the sources’ or ‘plan your answer’ are being invited to fail
  • Extended analytical writing is not impossibly difficult — it is insufficiently taught
  • High-quality analytical writing is the preserve of all learners, not just the highest attainers

Section B: Why Analytical Writing Matters Adran B: Pam Mae Ysgrifennu Dadansoddol yn Bwysig

🇬🇧 English

Counsell argues that extended writing is not simply a task pupils do at the end of a topic. It is a pedagogical tool — a means of developing and sustaining historical thinking, not merely recording it. Key Element 5 of the National Curriculum acts as a reminder that organisational demands must be explicitly taught, not assumed.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Mae Counsell yn dadlau nad tasg syml y mae disgyblion yn ei gwneud ar ddiwedd pwnc yw ysgrifennu estynedig. Mae’n offeryn addysgegol — modd o ddatblygu a chynnal meddwl hanesyddol, nid dim ond ei gofnodi. Mae Elfen Allweddol 5 yn atgoffa athrawon bod yn rhaid dysgu gofynion trefnadol yn benodol.

Three Key Elements of Organisation · Tair Elfen Allweddol Trefniadaeth

KE 5a

Recall, select and organise historical information. Selecting the relevant and rejecting the irrelevant must become explicit learning objectives — not assumptions. / Cofio, dewis a threfnu gwybodaeth hanesyddol. Rhaid i ddewis fod yn amcan dysgu penodol.

KE 5b

Use substantive vocabulary as tools for classifying information. Terms like ‘peasantry’, ‘economic’, ‘nobility’ enable precision. Pupils need to deploy them critically, not parrot definitions. / Geirfa sylweddol fel offer ar gyfer dosbarthu gwybodaeth yn fanwl gywir.

KE 5c

Communicate using different forms: narratives, descriptions, analyses, explanations. Each form requires pupils to arrange facts and ideas in a different way. Moving between forms is itself a demanding cognitive act. / Cyfathrebu gan ddefnyddio ffurfiau gwahanol: naratifau, dadansoddiadau, esboniadau.

📌 Writing and Thinking are Mutually Reinforcing · Mae Ysgrifennu a Meddwl yn Atgyfnerthu Ei Gilydd

Understanding of cause (KE2b) and skill in analytical composition (KE5c) are not separate. Teaching pupils to analyse causation also means teaching them to organise a written causal argument. Attention to structure and genre increases pupils’ meta-thinking — their ability to think about what they are doing and why. / Mae dealltwriaeth o achosiaeth a sgil mewn cyfansoddiad dadansoddol yn atgyfnerthu ei gilydd. Mae sylw i strwythur a genre yn cynyddu meta-feddwl.

“The leap from information-gathering into extended prose still represents several moves that many activities fail to acknowledge.”

— Counsell (1997), p. 10

Section C: Why Is Analytical Writing Difficult? Adran C: Pam Mae Ysgrifennu Dadansoddol yn Anodd?

🇬🇧 English

Counsell identifies five specific challenges that make analytical writing harder than narrative or descriptive writing. These affect all pupils but become prohibitive for lower attainers — leading to copying, drift into irrelevance, or the ‘self-fulfilling prophecy’ that some pupils cannot write analytically. Understanding these challenges is the first step toward designing teaching that overcomes them.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Mae Counsell yn nodi pum her benodol sy’n gwneud ysgrifennu dadansoddol yn anoddach nag ysgrifennu naratif. Maent yn effeithio ar bob disgybl ond maent yn dod yn rhai gwaharddiadol i ddisgyblion o gyrhaeddiad is. Mae deall yr heriau hyn yn gam cyntaf tuag at gynllunio addysgu sy’n eu goresgyn.

i

Memory and Concentration

Cof a Chanolbwyntio

To make more than one point requires retaining multiple issues in short-term memory. Even placing sources in front of pupils does not solve this — they must still hold and relate ideas. Lower attainers can lose sight of the question as new information crowds out old. Joining up thinking can outstrip available concentration.

ii

Relevance and Selection

Perthnasedd a Dewis

Pupils must distinguish between topic-relevance (this fact is about trade) and question-relevance (this fact supports the specific point I am making). Many lower attainers cannot ‘see’ that some facts will do and some will not. Selection must be explicitly taught — it does not happen automatically, even for high attainers.

iii

Sorting

Didoli

History’s subject matter must be shaped into meaningful structures. Sorting overlaps with relevance but creates additional demands: it requires attaching labels to groups using abstract concepts (political, economic, social; short-term, long-term). Pupils not taught to sort cannot analyse or evaluate with precision — they have no conscious problem-solving strategies.

iv

General and Particular

Cyffredinol a Pharticular

Even simple analysis requires distinguishing between general claims (big points) and supporting examples (little points). Where answers are incoherent, it is often because pupils fail to see — let alone show — the difference in status between a general claim and a supporting example. The problem runs in both directions: only examples, or only generalisations.

v

The Language of Discourse

Iaith y Traethawd

Even where pupils have sorted and organised their points, many are prevented from turning them into sustained discourse because they lack connective language: contrastive connectives (however, nevertheless), causal connectives (therefore, as a result), and temporal connectives (eventually, meanwhile). This is a teachable skill — not a reflection of intelligence.

⚠

Counsell warns: do not confuse the five challenges. A pupil who lists facts indiscriminately has a sorting problem, not just a relevance problem. Precise diagnosis leads to precise teaching. / Mae Counsell yn rhybuddio: peidiwch â drysu’r pum her.

Section D: Practical Approaches to Teaching Analytical Writing Adran D: Dulliau Ymarferol ar gyfer Addysgu Ysgrifennu Dadansoddol

📄 About Section 4 of the pamphlet · Am Adran 4

Section 4 is the most extensive and practically focused part of the pamphlet. Counsell presents ten strategies under three broad headings. None is intended to be used in fixed sequence; each addresses specific challenges and can be adapted for any age or ability. The acid test for any strategy: ‘Will this open up pupils’ thinking, or will it close thinking down?’ / Y prawf asid: “A fydd hyn yn agor meddwl disgyblion, neu’n ei gau i lawr?”

📚
Group A — Sorting Strategies (i–iv) Grwp A — Strategaethau Didoli · Challenges ii, iii, iv

Sorting strategies give pupils a concrete, kinaesthetic experience of the decisions that analytical writing requires before they write a single sentence. / Mae strategaethau didoli yn rhoi profiad concrid i ddisgyblion o’r penderfyniadau cyn iddynt ysgrifennu un frawddeg.

(i) Sorting with given criteria iiiii
Didoli â meini prawf penodol

Pupils place points under given headings (e.g. causes/consequences; political/economic/social). Even apparently simple sorting becomes complex when points overlap between categories — which is the teaching point. The difficulty itself generates the discussion. / Mae disgyblion yn gosod pwyntiau o dan benawdau penodol. Mae’r anhawster ei hun yn bwynt addysgu.

(ii) Sorting using more difficult abstract concepts iiiii
Didoli gan ddefnyddio cysyniadau haniaethol mwy anodd

Pupils move from concrete to abstract sorting: from ‘rich and poor’ to ‘gentry’ and ‘nobility’; from vague groupings to ‘political’, ‘economic’, ‘social’. This develops the conceptual tools needed to sort — and therefore to write — with precision. It is not vocabulary instruction for its own sake. / Mae disgyblion yn symud o’r concrid i’r haniaethol: datblygu’r offer cysyniadol sydd eu hangen i ddidoli ac ysgrifennu.

(iii) Choosing their own sorting criteria iii
Dewis eu meini prawf didoli eu hunain

Pupils devise their own categories, then compare with given ones. This reveals which criteria work and which do not — and crucially, that different sets of criteria will cut across each other. For example, sorting causes of the Normans’ victory by ‘luck’, ‘skill’ and ‘timing’ produces different groupings from ‘William’ vs ‘Harold’. / Mae disgyblion yn dyfeisio eu categorïau eu hunain, yna’n cymharu. Mae gwahanol feini prawf yn croestorri ei gilydd.

(iv) Making items for sorting iiiii
Gwneud eitemau i’w didoli

Pupils create the sorting cards themselves — summarising a set of causes, for example — before sorting them. A ten-card limit forces succinct, discriminating phrasing. This also develops note-taking as a form of sorting, which Counsell argues should be taught explicitly from KS3. / Mae disgyblion yn creu’r cardiau eu hunain cyn eu didoli. Mae terfyn deg cerdyn yn gorfodi phrasio cryno.

📈
Group B — Arranging Strategies (v–vii) Grwp B — Strategaethau Trefnu · Challenges iii, iv

Arranging strategies move from sorting to creating relational structures — showing how ideas link, overlap or are subordinate. These are particularly effective for pupils who can select relevant material but cannot yet see how to organise it into a written argument. / Mae strategaethau trefnu yn symud o ddidoli i greu strwythurau cysylltiol.

(v) Diagrams iiiiv
Diagramau

Causation diagrams, Venn diagrams, ripple diagrams, and ‘It depends’ continuum diagrams all help pupils create relational structures before writing. The Venn diagram allows pupils to manage overlapping categories — and the process of creating it teaches pupils to question whether their original sorting criteria were adequate. / Mae diagramau achosiaeth, Venn, crych a ‘mae’n dibynnu’ i gyd yn helpu disgyblion creu strwythurau cysylltiol cyn ysgrifennu.

(vi) Contrasting writing structures iiiiv
Strwythurau ysgrifennu cyferbyniol

Pupils are shown at least two different ways of organising the same material (e.g. moving ‘vertically’ — covering all changes in one period — vs ‘horizontally’ — explaining the reason for each change as you describe it). Seeing that there are multiple valid structures is itself a significant insight. The comparison becomes a teaching conversation about argument and organisation. / Dangosir o leiaf ddwy ffordd wahanol o drefnu’r un deunydd i ddisgyblion.

(vii) ‘Big points’ and ‘little points’ iv
Pwyntiau mawr a phwyntiau bach

Lower-attaining pupils are given the ‘big points’ (general claims) first, then search for ‘little points’ (supporting examples). More confident pupils find their own pattern. This directly addresses challenge (iv) by making the general/particular distinction a concrete, physical activity before it becomes a writing expectation. / Rhoddir y ‘pwyntiau mawr’ (honiadau cyffredinol) i ddisgyblion o gyrhaeddiad is yn gyntaf, yna maent yn chwilio am ‘bwyntiau bach’.

💬
Group C — Language of Discourse (viii–x) Grwp C — Iaith y Traethawd · Challenge v

These strategies address challenge (v) — the language of analytical prose. They recognise that pupils may have excellent ideas that they cannot yet turn into sustained argument because they lack linguistic tools. / Mae’r strategaethau hyn yn mynd i’r afael â’r iaith ar gyfer rhyddiaith ddadansoddol.

(viii) ‘Clever starters’ — sentence stems and connective menus v
Dechreuadau clyfar — boncyffion brawddegau a rhestrau cysyllteiriau

A permanent display of connectives and sentence-opening stems from which pupils can choose, depending on the type of historical problem. These are not crutches — they free pupils to concentrate on higher-order thinking (the historical argument) by removing the lower-order burden (how to start). As Counsell says: ‘Sometimes, we need to do the lower-order thinking for pupils so that they are freed to do the higher-order thinking for themselves.’ / Arddangosiad parhaol o gysyllteiriau a dechreuadau brawddeg. Nid bagiau cymorth yw’r rhain.

Counsell’s ‘Clever Starters’ examples / Enghreifftiau o ‘Dechreuadau Clyfar’ Counsell:

We cannot be sure about … because …
I don’t know if … because …
This source tells me that …
This source suggests that …
On the one hand …
On the other hand …
A source which backs me up on this is …
However, … / Nevertheless, …
(ix) Writing frames ivv
Fframweithiau ysgrifennu

Structured templates that model the genre and language features of a particular type of analytical writing. Counsell stresses (following Wray and Lewis) that frames must never become fixed — pupils must be weaned off them. Used judiciously, a writing frame isolates a key teaching point about structure and models it for the pupil who might not otherwise succeed. / Templedi strwythuredig sy’n modelu nodweddion genre. Rhaid i fframweithiau byth fod yn sefydlog — mae’n rhaid cael gwared ar y cymorth yn raddol.

(x) Modelling style v
Modelu arddull

Pupils — especially able ones — need exposure to increasingly demanding and varied examples of historical writing: academic, journalistic, scholarly. Short extracts from historians’ articles can be highly motivating at KS3. ‘Whole school’ language policies should consider explicitly how explanatory genres in history and science converge or differ. This cannot be left to chance. / Mae angen i ddisgyblion abl gael eu hamlygu i enghreifftiau mwy heriol o ysgrifennu hanesyddol.

Section E: Timing, Scale and Progression Adran E: Amseriad, Maint a Dilyniant

🇬🇧 English

Section 5 addresses a practical question: how do you fit extended analytical writing into a crowded scheme of work? Counsell’s answer is that this question rests on a misunderstanding of what analytical writing does. It is not a large undertaking bolted onto the end of a topic — it can be done at many different scales, and time spent building toward it is an investment, not a cost.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Cymraeg

Mae Adran 5 yn mynd i’r afael â chwestiwn ymarferol: sut mae ffitio ysgrifennu dadansoddol estynedig i gynllun gwaith gorlawn? Ateb Counsell yw bod y cwestiwn hwn yn seiliedig ar gamddealltwriaeth. Nid ymgymeriad mawr wedi’i atodi ar ddiwedd pwnc yw ysgrifennu dadansoddol — mae’n fuddsoddiad, nid cost.

BEFORE WRITING · CYN YSGRIFENNU

Role play, film, imaginative activities, structured discussion — all effective forms of preparation. Not distractions from writing but essential prerequisites. Where pupils are familiar with material and emotionally engaged, they have a stake in the outcome and write with flair. / Chwarae rôl, ffilm, gweithgareddau dychmygus — nid testynau gwyriad ond rhagofynion hanfodol.

DURING WRITING · YN YSTOD YSGRIFENNU

Manage the tension between structure and independence. ‘Minimum success assured’ + ‘considerable learning risks’. Crucially: sometimes give higher attainers more structure (not less) to model a more demanding form of discourse. Different levels of scaffold for different groups. / Weithiau rhowch fwy o strwythur i ddisgyblion o gyrhaeddiad uwch i fodelu ffurf fwy heriol.

AFTER WRITING · AR ÔL YSGRIFENNU

Use writing as a teaching resource in its own right: colour-code different parts of an argument; cut paragraphs and reassemble; find and underline ‘big points’. The most significant audience for pupil writing should be the pupil themselves. / Defnyddio ysgrifennu fel adnodd addysgu ynddo’i hun: cod-lliwio dadl; torri paragraffau; tanlinellu pwyntiau mawr.

“If a department chooses to spend three weeks on the causes of one event, it is an investment that will save time later: pupils will transfer skills to subsequent work, and they will acquire secure knowledge from so much engagement with a set of issues.”

— Counsell (1997), p. 10 — the investment argument / y ddadl fuddsoddiad

🔄 Principles for Progression Across a Key Stage · Egwyddorion Dilyniant

  • Repetition of core techniques — so pupils become fluent and can eventually choose their own / Ailadrodd technegau craidd — fel y daw disgyblion yn rhugl
  • Phased introduction of new, more challenging forms of sorting and organisation / Cyflwyniad cyfnodol o ffurfiau newydd fwy heriol
  • Explicit opportunities to analyse their own writing — and each other’s — discussing structural and stylistic features / Cyfleoedd penodol i ddadansoddi eu hysgrifennu eu hunain
  • Careful balance between structure and independence at every stage — not a simple withdrawal of support / Cydbwysedd gofalus rhwng strwythur ac annibyniaeth
  • Simply doing a lot of extended writing does not in itself produce progress. Systematic, reflective, explicit teaching does. / Nid yw gwneud llawer o ysgrifennu yn cynhyrchu cynnydd. Mae addysgu systematig, myfyriol a phriodol yn gwneud hynny.

Section F: Key Principles & Final Reflection Adran F: Egwyddorion Allweddol ac Adlewyrchiad Terfynol

💡 Counsell’s Pamphlet Resists a Checklist · Mae Pamffled Counsell yn Gwrthsefyll Rhestr Wirio

Its deepest argument is about a way of thinking about teaching, not a set of techniques to apply. These eight principles summarise the spirit of her argument and should inform every writing task you design. / Mae ei ddadl ddyfnaf am ffordd o feddwl am addysgu, nid set o dechnegau i’w cymhwyso.

🎯

The Acid Test · Y Prawf Asid

Before any strategy: “Will this open up pupils’ thinking, or will it close thinking down?” Mechanistic tasks may produce writing but not analytical thinking. Open-ended tasks without structure leave pupils unable to start. / Cyn unrhyw strategaeth: “A fydd hyn yn agor meddwl disgyblion, neu’n ei gau i lawr?”

🔬

Diagnosis Before Prescription · Diagnosis cyn Presgripsiwn

Knowing a pupil “can’t write analytically” is not useful. Knowing they have a sorting problem, or a language problem, or a general/particular problem, immediately suggests the teaching response. Precise diagnosis = precise teaching. / Mae gwybod bod gan ddisgybl broblem ddidoli, neu broblem iaith, yn awgrymu’r ymateb addysgu ar unwaith.

🏗

Structure Enables · Mae Strwythur yn Galluogi

Writing frames and sentence starters are not crutches. Used well, they free pupils to concentrate on higher-order thinking by removing lower-order burdens. The goal is always to wean pupils off support — but prematurely removing it prevents progress. / Mae fframweithiau ysgrifennu yn rhyddhau disgyblion i ganolbwyntio ar feddwl lefel uwch.

🔄

Writing and Thinking are Inseparable · Anwahanadwy

Analytical writing tasks do not just assess historical understanding — they develop it. A department that treats writing only as an assessment outcome misses the teaching power that writing tasks contain. / Nid yw tasgau ysgrifennu dadansoddol yn asesu dealltwriaeth yn unig — maent yn ei datblygu.

📈

Progression Requires Planning · Cynllunio Bwriadol

High-quality analytical writing does not happen by magic or from spending lots of time writing essays. It requires systematic repetition of core techniques; phased introduction of new forms; explicit teaching of selection, sorting, and the language of discourse. / Nid yw ysgrifennu dadansoddol o ansawdd uchel yn digwydd trwy hud.

⚖

Avoid Both Extremes · Osgowch y Ddau Eithaf

Experienced teachers avoid two failures: mechanistic/formulaic work on one hand, and activities so open-ended that pupils cannot find structure on the other. Both produce the same result: pupils who cannot write analytically. The professional challenge is holding the middle ground. / Mae’r ddau eithaf yn cynhyrchu’r un canlyniad.

💰

Extended Writing is an Investment · Buddsoddiad

Time spent building toward a piece of extended writing generates secure knowledge through deep engagement; produces transferable skills; and builds the meta-cognitive capacity pupils need to become genuinely independent historical thinkers. / Mae amser a dreuliwyd yn adeiladu tuag at ysgrifennu estynedig yn cynhyrchu gwybodaeth sicr ac yn adeiladu gallu meta-wybyddol.

👥

All Pupils Can · Gall Pob Disgybl

Counsell’s central and most provocative claim. Lower attainers are not incapable of analytical writing — they are under-taught. The vicious circle must be broken by deliberate, systematic, explicit teaching of the specific skills involved. / Nid yw disgyblion o gyrhaeddiad is yn methu ysgrifennu dadansoddol. Nid ydynt wedi’u dysgu’n ddigonol.

Task F · Tasg F — Final Reflection (350–450 words)

Write a sustained, analytical response to Counsell’s central claim — itself using the principles she describes: clear opening position, specific evidence, acknowledgement of counter-argument, and a developed conclusion that goes beyond restating your opening. / Ysgrifennwch ymateb cynaliadwy, dadansoddol i brif honiad Counsell — gan ddefnyddio’r egwyddorion y mae hi’n eu disgrifio.

Claim to respond to: “Pupils of almost all abilities can write critically and analytically, but we must teach them.” Your response should: (i) state your position clearly at the opening; (ii) use at least two pieces of specific evidence from the pamphlet; (iii) draw on at least one observation from your placement; (iv) acknowledge a counter-argument; (v) reach a conclusion that does more than restate your opening. / Honiad i ymateb iddo: “Gall disgyblion o bron pob gallu ysgrifennu’n feirniadol a dadansoddol, ond mae’n rhaid i ni eu dysgu nhw.”
